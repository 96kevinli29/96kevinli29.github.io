G20 and rich countries (default weights)

  • The Nordic countries lead. Norway is 1st, Denmark 3rd, Sweden 4th and Finland 5th. They use a lot of electricity (cheap hydropower, cold winters), live long, are safe, and send many young people to university. Luxembourg is 1st on GDP per person and 2nd on living standards; Switzerland goes from 3rd to 6th.
  • China sits further above the trend line than any other economy. It is 22nd of these 27 on GDP per person and 17th on living standards. Food scores full marks; 94% of people feel safe walking alone at night and there are 0.5 homicides per 100,000, for a safety score of 96; tertiary enrolment is 77%; the average person travels about 960 km a year by high-speed and urban rail, behind only Japan and France. Floor area (about 36 m² per person) and health pull the score down.
  • South Korea (16th → 10th) and Japan (18th → 13th) rise markedly: dense rail, long lives, safe streets. Japan's tertiary enrolment of 65% is the lowest among the high-income economies here.
  • The United States is 4th on GDP per person and 16th on living standards. Cars, floor area and education are near full marks, but only 71% of people feel safe walking alone at night and the homicide rate of 5.8 per 100,000 is nearly 30 times Japan's; there is little high-speed or urban rail; life expectancy trails other rich economies. Saudi Arabia (8th → 18th), the United Kingdom (14th → 19th) and Iceland (5th → 12th) also fall. Britain uses about 4,600 kWh of electricity per person, the least of the high-income economies here.
  • Canada (11th → 8th) is the only North American economy in the top ten. Like the US it has many cars, big homes (about 59 m² per person) and high electricity use, but it is safer (2.0 homicides per 100,000 against 5.8) and its people live three years longer. Its weak points are the lack of high-speed rail and safety below Western European levels (about twice the EU homicide rate).
  • Russia, Argentina, Brazil, Mexico and South Africa all score low on safety, and Argentina, Brazil and Mexico have only about 18–22 m² of floor area per person (estimates). Indonesia, India and South Africa take the last three places, far behind on electricity, meat and eggs, and cars; India has about 11 m² of floor area per person and tertiary enrolment of 34%.

Across all 79 economies

  • Resource and financial economies fall furthest. Guyana, which began large-scale oil production only in 2019, is 7th on GDP per person and 72nd on living standards. Macao (3rd → 25th), Qatar (4th → 23rd), Brunei (8th → 42nd), Hong Kong (12th → 22nd) and Singapore (1st → 9th) all drop. Hong Kong's floor area of about 16 m² per person is the smallest of any economy with data.
  • China is also furthest above the trend among all 79: 50th on GDP per person, 18th on living standards.
  • South Africa, Nigeria and Pakistan come last. India is 75th. South Africa's GDP per person is 1.4 times India's, but it does worse on safety, health and education.
